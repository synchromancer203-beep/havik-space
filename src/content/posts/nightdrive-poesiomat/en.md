It's been 8 years since I wrote Nightdrive.

It was my last year of high school. Nightdrive was the first track I wrote, produced and released in full. It was also the first synthwave track in Vietnam (I've waited 8 years and no other musician has come forward with a Vietnamese synthwave track made before it).

Honestly, getting Corrupted Moonlight's music to reach people has been a hard problem ever since the project began. Synthwave, at heart, is music that recreates how people living in the 1980s dreamed of the future (flying cars, neon cities and so on). Because it carries a vibe that is nostalgic yet full of science fiction, it isn't nearly as easy to get into as other popular genres. Listeners need some connection to 1980s Western pop culture to really understand and "feel" synthwave (I wasn't even born then, let alone lived through it).

Yesterday, Derek and I, the two representatives of Corrupted Moonlight, were invited to 'Poesiomat Echoes from Czechia', the launch of the first Poesiomat in Hanoi, organised by the Czech Centre in Hanoi and Complex 01. The Poesiomat is introduced as a "poetry machine", a one-of-a-kind outdoor sound player from the Czech Republic that brings together memories of the past and culture for the future, an effort to preserve and spread culture in the most forward-looking way. For this one-off Poesiomat, the first made just for Hanoi, the organisers selected 20 works, poems and sounds typical of Hanoi and the Czech Republic.

Complex 01 itself was one of the first projects to turn an old factory into a complex that keeps the traces of time while growing modern culture and entertainment for young people in Hanoi.

Standing in front of the Poesiomat and hearing the first track I ever wrote play from the machine was a feeling that's hard to describe. Part of it was pride, but the bigger part was realising where Nightdrive belongs. The track playing through a poetry machine, in a cultural complex: all of them firsts, bringing the past and the future together.

On behalf of Corrupted Moonlight, I'd like to thank the organisers, the Czech Centre in Hanoi and Complex 01 for putting their hearts into bringing this project to Vietnam, and for giving my "first-born" a place on this meaningful cultural journey.
