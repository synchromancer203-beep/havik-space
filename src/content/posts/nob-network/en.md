Every day, around 60,000 tracks made entirely by AI (with no human creating them) are uploaded to Deezer. That is more than a third of all new music uploaded worldwide each day, up 400% in a single year. Meanwhile, 97% of the people surveyed couldn't tell AI music from music written by humans (figures from Deezer).

I'm pretty good at spotting AI music. I can even hear which model generated a track, probably because I listen to a lot of music every day and produce music myself. AI music sounds full, perfectly produced, right on trend. Nothing to complain about, but nothing much stays with you either.

We live in an age where music is optimised not to be skipped. Not to be remembered. Not to change anyone. Just optimised to keep the algorithm running.

I'm a small-time boss at a company that applies AI and trains people in it (a company starting with "i", which shall remain nameless). I use AI and generative AI in my daily work, I teach basic AI, and I have a personal AI assistant nagging me about tasks all day long. Every day I hear "AI", "AI", "AI" a hundred times or so around the office. I watch the kid on our marketing team wire up N8N and Make nodes to push hundreds of pieces of content out to every platform each day, and that system reminds me of electronic instruments (synth modules, drum machines and so on) patched together with cables on the round table at Otto Bar every Friday night, at the synth jams of OPEN (I'm one of its four members). I think: if this kid came to Otto to patch synths and play, he'd love it, wouldn't he?

I think of the friends I play music with. They're all a fair bit older than me; Derek Bùi is even the same age as my company's CEO. Every other Friday night, the four of us in OPEN stand in front of a table strewn with mini synthesizers, a modular rack and loop stations, and make music on the spot at Otto Bar while the guests drink and talk. No prepared files, no fixed structure, no Ctrl Z, we don't even talk to each other; we just call out the key and tempo. Every decision is made in milliseconds. Turning a knob or plugging in a cable has consequences right away, and nothing can be undone.

![Synth jam: a Roland TR-8, a Roland SE-02 and other electronic instruments patched together with cables](./otto-synth-jam.webp)

Playing, I thought: this is something AI can't do!

I did try generating the kind of music we play with Suno AI, using a very detailed prompt of mine. What it gave me back was a soft EDM track with a female vocal, really well mixed, but on the whole very mid, and of course not what I meant. Not because AI lacks technique or music theory, but because the two have completely different aims: one chases the perfect number, the other chases the collective feeling of the present moment and lets the mistakes go. We know that you know the artists in front of you are really making what you're hearing, right now, and will never make it exactly the same - ever - again.

Hanoi has always had groups who play like this and individuals working underground like this, but they haven't been connected to each other or known, like synthesizers sitting unpatched in the dark.

So I founded NÓB Network, (possibly) the first network bringing together live electronic artists in Vietnam. Not to start yet another record label, not to build a media channel that promotes whichever artists throw money at it, but to lay the groundwork for something that already vaguely exists but doesn't have a name yet. So electronic artists have more places to play, and people looking for artists know where they play.

On 20 March, NÓB Network officially launches.

The event is called NÓB: INIT, after the "INIT preset" on modern synthesizers: the starting point from which a player shapes a new sound, the beginning of tracks they're proud to have built from zero. Quy Trong Do plays modular synth. TuanSS plays live looping and beatbox. OPEN Project plays a synth jam. And before that, a screening of TESTRUN, a live session project by Corrupted Moonlight that NÓB made before NÓB took shape.

I've always been taught that long-winded content like this doesn't perform on any platform these days. But if you've read this far, you have all my respect, and

Otto Bar, 18 Thanh Niên. 7 pm on 20 March, see you there.
